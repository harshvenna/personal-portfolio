SupplyChainAI — Factory Reallocation & Shipping Optimization

A decision-intelligence platform for a candy distributor's 5-factory network — combining predictive ML, multi-objective optimization, Monte Carlo simulation, and SHAP explainability behind a 12-tab Streamlit dashboard.

10,194 orders analyzed 5 factories · 15 products 2024 – 2026 Python 3.12

Problem

The distributor assigned products to factories using static legacy rules — resulting in suboptimal shipping distances, uneven utilization (one factory, Lot's O' Nuts, handled 55.7% of all units), avoidable margin erosion, and no way to simulate a better allocation before committing to it.

Architecture

A modular pipeline: data_pipeline.py engineers 45 features from the raw order data, ml_models.py trains and benchmarks a 6-model suite, optimization_engine.py scores reallocation scenarios and runs Monte Carlo simulation, and shap_explainer.py generates global/local explanations — all surfaced through a 12-tab Streamlit dashboard (Executive Overview, EDA, Factory Analytics, Route Intelligence, ML Models, Optimization Simulator, Recommendations, Monte Carlo, SHAP, Factory Map, Risk Analytics, Executive Report).

Optimization framework

Key decisions & honest limitations

  • Built as an academic/independent project using the Nassau Candy sample dataset — a demonstration of pipeline design and operations-research methodology, not a live client engagement.
  • The top-ranked recommendation (SweeTARTS, Interior region: Sugar Shack → Secret Factory) scored 77.6 / 100 on the composite objective — reallocation, not a black-box output.

02 / 02 Illustrative / Synthetic Data

Workforce Engagement & Burnout Intelligence Platform

A 14-module enterprise-style Streamlit suite covering engagement, burnout risk, career stagnation, attrition early-warning, ML prediction, SHAP explainability, and an HR scenario simulator.

1,470 employees · 31 fields 14 modules 2025 – 2026 Python · Streamlit

Problem & data disclosure

HR teams need to move past static headcount reporting toward proactively flagging engagement, burnout, and attrition risk. The underlying dataset is structurally modeled on the public IBM HR Analytics Attrition dataset and used here as illustrative/synthetic data to demonstrate platform architecture and methodology — every page in the app carries an "Illustrative Data" badge, and it is not real personnel data from any employer.

Architecture

A single, sectioned app.py spanning a shared Foundation layer plus 13 feature modules: Executive Overview, Employee Experience EDA, Engagement Intelligence, Burnout Risk Center, Workload Stress Analytics, Department Intelligence, Career & Growth Analytics, Attrition Early Warning, a Machine Learning Hub, an Explainable AI Center (SHAP), K-Means Workforce Segmentation, an HR Scenario Simulator, and Workforce Risk / Manager Action Center.

Results

0.77–0.80
AUC — attrition model (real label)
5
Engineered KPIs, fully transparent formulas
0.27
Silhouette score — K-Means segmentation

Key decisions & honest limitations

  • Engineered KPIs (Engagement Index, Burnout Risk Score, Workforce Health Score) are documented weighted formulas, never a black-box score.
  • Burnout/engagement classifiers deliberately exclude their own formula's direct inputs to avoid circular prediction — expect a modest AUC of ~0.55–0.65 there, by design, not a bug. The attrition model (a real label) performs better at ~0.77–0.80.
  • The HR Scenario Simulator re-runs the exact same formulas used elsewhere in the app — a single source of truth rather than a separate toy model.
  • K-Means segmentation reports an honest ~0.27 silhouette score rather than implying stronger natural clustering than the data supports; the HR department (63 employees) is treated as a small-n slice with wider error margins.
